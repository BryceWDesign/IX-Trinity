# IX-Trinity Claim Boundaries

IX-Trinity is a source-available technical evaluation repository for a bounded,
measurement-first pulsed-energy architecture.

The project investigates whether a three-sector, evidence-gated storage and
discharge architecture can reduce impossible single-path demands by distributing
conduction, storage, timing, sensing, discharge, fault isolation, and evidence
handling across isolated modules.

## Core pain points

IX-Trinity is organized around three linked engineering pain points:

1. Conduct without resistance.
2. Store without breakdown.
3. Confine without failing.

These are research targets and architectural drivers, not proven achieved
capabilities.

## What IX-Trinity may claim in v0.1

IX-Trinity v0.1 may claim that it defines a low-energy, software-modeled,
evidence-gated architecture for:

- three isolated storage sectors;
- conservative voltage and energy limits;
- precharge and dump discipline;
- no-backfeed rules;
- phase-authority records;
- sensor-truth checks;
- per-sector energy accounting;
- dummy-load baseline comparison;
- passive coupon/witness cartridge records;
- evidence bundles and receipts;
- acceptance, rejection, and claim-demotion logic;
- human-review-required scale-up gates.

## What IX-Trinity must not claim in v0.1

IX-Trinity v0.1 must not claim:

- solved fusion;
- plasma confinement success;
- high-field success;
- production readiness;
- certification;
- defense approval;
- industrial deployment readiness;
- high-voltage hardware readiness;
- superconducting hardware proof;
- breakthrough supercapacitor performance;
- safe real-world pulsed-power operation;
- operational safety;
- unlimited conduction;
- unlimited storage;
- unlimited confinement;
- autonomous scale-up approval.

## v0.1 operating posture

IX-Trinity v0.1 is intentionally conservative.

The first target is a low-energy tri-sector storage/discharge testbed model.
The v0.1 architecture exists to prove governance, evidence handling, storage
discipline, timing discipline, safety refusal, and claim boundaries before any
higher-energy work is considered.

## Hard claim rule

No IX-Trinity claim is accepted without evidence.

A run, shot, test, or simulation must be rejected or marked inconclusive when
required evidence is missing, stale, saturated, inconsistent, outside policy, or
not bound into a reviewable evidence package.

## Scale-up rule

Software may recommend scale-up review.

Software may not approve scale-up by itself.

Any increase in voltage, stored energy, switching severity, cartridge severity,
plasma relevance, high-field relevance, superconducting hardware relevance, or
industrial relevance requires human review and a recorded scale-up decision.

## Falsification rule

IX-Trinity must be easy to falsify.

If one-sector or two-sector baselines perform as well as a three-sector topology,
the three-sector superiority claim must be demoted.

If dummy-load controls produce the same apparent result as coupon tests, the
coupon claim must be rejected or marked inconclusive.

If energy accounting does not close, energy-related claims must be rejected.

If timing proof is missing, phase-related claims must be rejected.

If sensor truth checks fail, measurement-related claims must be rejected.

## Safety posture

IX-Trinity is not a build authorization.

IX-Trinity is not a safety case.

IX-Trinity is not a certification package.

IX-Trinity is not an operational guarantee.

IX-Trinity is not a substitute for qualified engineering review, laboratory
safety review, electrical safety review, pulsed-power expertise, cryogenic safety
review, pressure-boundary review, or legal/regulatory review.

## Public positioning

IX-Trinity is public for technical evaluation.

IX-Trinity is source-available.

IX-Trinity is not open source.
